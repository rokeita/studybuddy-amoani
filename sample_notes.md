# Sample study notes: Fundamentals of Managerial Economics (Lecture 1)

## What is Economics?
- Economics studies how society manages its scarce resources.
- Scarcity means society has limited resources and cannot produce all the goods and services people wish to have.
- Because of scarcity, every decision involves a trade-off: choosing one thing means giving up another.
- Example: a food processing company that spends more on advertising has fewer resources left to invest in R&D.

## Who is a Manager?
- A manager is a person who directs resources to achieve a stated goal.
- Managers direct the efforts of others, purchase inputs used to produce goods and services, and make decisions such as product price and quality.

## What is Managerial Economics?
- It is the study of how to direct scarce resources in the way that most efficiently achieves an optimal managerial decision.
- Example decisions a manager makes: whether to purchase tins from other manufacturers or produce them within the firm; what quantity to produce and at what price to sell; how many workers to hire and how to compensate them; how the actions of rival firms affect these decisions.

## The Ten Principles of Economics
How people and businesses make decisions:
1. People and businesses face trade-offs.
2. The cost of something is what you give up to get it (opportunity cost).
3. Rational people and businesses think at the margin.
4. People and businesses respond to incentives.
How people and businesses interact:
5. Trade can make everyone better off.
6. Markets are usually a good way to organize economic activity.
7. Governments can sometimes improve market outcomes.
How the economy as a whole works:
8. A country's standard of living depends on its ability to produce goods and services.
9. Prices rise when the government prints too much money.
10. Society faces a short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment.

## The Business Environment
- Business activity involves taking a series of inputs and producing an output (a physical good or service).
- B2C: a business sells goods and services to the final consumer.
- B2B: a business sells a good to another business.
- C2C: consumers trade with each other through platforms such as eBay, Amazon and Tonaton.
- The transformation process: businesses take factor inputs, process them, and produce outputs which are then sold.
- The factors of production are land, labour, capital and enterprise.
